# KOREAN_DRAFT_ORIGIN_RUNTIME

## why this exists
The main Korean lyric problem was not lack of data. It was the birthplace of the first draft.
When the model starts with a concept summary and fills Verse/Chorus slots, it produces clean but dead Korean.
This runtime forces Korean lyric-centered songs to begin in a speaker's messy speech.

## mandatory flow
1. User premise inventory
2. Speaker pressure card
3. Ugly monologue
4. Hook candidate mining
5. Chorus-first carving
6. Verse as evidence
7. V2 as worsening/disclosure
8. Bridge as defense rupture
9. Final as defense shift
10. Korean morpheme repair
11. Phoneme/topline repair
12. Cue pass

## speaker pressure card
Before any lyric line, lock:
- what the speaker wants
- what the speaker denies
- what the body/action already reveals
- what object carries the lie
- who the speaker imagines as listener
- what word/image must be saved for the ending
- what stock scenery is banned

## ugly monologue
15-25 sentences. No line breaks as lyrics. No section labels. No poetic polish.
It must include:
- excuses
- contradiction
- small repairs
- action and sensory detail
- one repeated phrase or pressure point
- a last-word or object seed

Bad monologue:
"나는 운동 중 딸기스무디를 생각하며 유혹과 보상을 느낀다."
Good monologue type:
"아니 그냥 목마른 거지. 운동했는데 물만 마셔야 된다는 법은 없잖아. 근데 왜 딸기부터 생각나냐. 아직 사지도 않았는데 컵이 손에 잡힌 것 같네."

Do not surface the monologue unless the user asks for staged work.

## hook candidate table
Mine 5-9 candidates:
- candidate
- speech act
- repeatability
- singable core
- final shift
- risk

Reject if it sounds like a thesis, ad slogan, or moral.

## carving rule
Chorus first.
Verse lines must prove why the chorus lie exists.
V2 must make the lie worse or more specific.
Bridge must puncture the lie.
Final must reuse, invert, or surrender the first hook.

## cue removal
Before adding cues, delete all cues and section labels and read the lyric.
If it needs the cue to explain feeling, return to monologue.
