For experimental harmony, reharmonization, and modal interchange

## SECTION 1 — SCALE SYSTEMS

### 1.1 Major Scale (Ionian Mode)
Formula: W W H W W W H (whole/half step pattern)
Intervals from root: 1 2 3 4 5 6 7
C major: C D E F G A B
Character: Bright, stable, foundational; default for pop, folk,
worship, classical major-key works.

### 1.2 Natural Minor Scale (Aeolian Mode)
Formula: W H W W H W W
Intervals: 1 2 b3 4 5 b6 b7
A minor: A B C D E F G
Character: Standard sadness, introspection; default for ballads,
gothic, alternative minor-key works.

### 1.3 Harmonic Minor Scale
Formula: W H W W H (W+H) H
Intervals: 1 2 b3 4 5 b6 7
A harmonic minor: A B C D E F G#
Character: Augmented 2nd between b6-7 creates Middle Eastern,
Romani, and flamenco color. Common in metal, film score, and
fusion contexts.

### 1.4 Melodic Minor Scale (Ascending Form)
Formula: W H W W W W H (ascending)
Intervals: 1 2 b3 4 5 6 7
A melodic minor: A B C D E F# G#
Character: Smooths the augmented 2nd of harmonic minor for
melodic ascending lines. Descending traditionally reverts to
natural minor; modern jazz uses melodic minor in both directions.

### 1.5 The Seven Diatonic Modes (Brightness Order)

Each mode shares the same notes as a parent major scale but
designates a different note as tonic, producing distinct
characters.

**Lydian** (brightest)
- Formula: W W W H W W H
- C Lydian: C D E F# G A B
- Characteristic note: #4 (raised fourth)
- Characteristic chord: II (D major in C Lydian)
- Mood: Mystical, floating, dreamy, hopeful
- Genres: Film score (mystery), dream pop, game music, fusion
- Common progression: I-II (F-G in C Lydian context)

**Ionian** (Major)
- Formula: W W H W W W H
- C Ionian: C D E F G A B
- Characteristic chord: I (C major)
- Mood: Bright, stable, definitive
- Genres: Pop, folk, worship, traditional Western

**Mixolydian**
- Formula: W W H W W H W
- C Mixolydian: C D E F G A Bb
- Characteristic note: b7 (lowered seventh)
- Characteristic chord: bVII (Bb major in C Mixolydian)
- Mood: Bluesy, heroic, modal-major, untethered
- Genres: Blues, classic rock, funk, Celtic folk
- Common progression: I-bVII-IV-I (C-Bb-F-C)

**Dorian**
- Formula: W H W W W H W
- C Dorian: C D Eb F G A Bb
- Characteristic note: natural 6 (raised sixth in minor context)
- Characteristic chord: IV major (F major in C Dorian)
- Mood: Modern dark, cool, jazz-funk inflected
- Genres: Jazz, funk, modal indie, Santana-style rock, Latin fusion
- Common progression: i-IV (Cm-F), i-IV-vii-i, i-bVII-IV-i

**Aeolian** (Natural Minor)
- Formula: W H W W H W W
- C Aeolian: C D Eb F G Ab Bb
- Characteristic chords: iv, bVI, bVII
- Mood: Standard sadness, gravitational pull
- Genres: Ballad, gothic rock, symphonic metal, K-ballad
- Common progression: i-VII-VI-VII, i-iv-VII, i-VI-III-VII

**Phrygian**
- Formula: H W W W H W W
- C Phrygian: C Db Eb F G Ab Bb
- Characteristic note: b2 (lowered second, half step from root)
- Characteristic chord: bII (Db major in C Phrygian)
- Mood: Spanish, severe darkness, exotic tension
- Genres: Flamenco, metal, drill, Middle Eastern fusion
- Common progression: i-bII (Em-F in E Phrygian), bII-bVII-i

**Locrian** (darkest)
- Formula: H W W H W W W
- C Locrian: C Db Eb F Gb Ab Bb
- Characteristic note: b5 (diminished fifth, no stable tonic)
- Characteristic chord: i° (diminished tonic)
- Mood: Unstable, avant-garde, theoretical
- Genres: Avant-garde, math rock, free jazz
- Note: Rarely used as full-song tonic due to tonal instability;
  more commonly encountered as transient color or modal passage.

### 1.6 Pentatonic and Blues Scales

**Major Pentatonic** (1 2 3 5 6)
C major pentatonic: C D E G A
- No avoid notes; "safe scale" for melodies
- Genres: Folk, country, gospel, J-pop melodies, K-pop choruses

**Minor Pentatonic** (1 b3 4 5 b7)
A minor pentatonic: A C D E G
- Universal vocal-friendly minor
- Genres: Rock, blues, hip-hop hooks, K-rap melody

**Blues Scale** (1 b3 4 b5 5 b7)
C blues: C Eb F Gb G Bb
- Blue note: b5 (Gb in C blues) — passing tone, never resting
- Genres: Blues, rock guitar, soul vocal runs

**Major Blues** (1 2 b3 3 5 6)
C major blues: C D Eb E G A
- Country, blues-rock major key
- Blue note: b3→3 chromatic passing

### 1.7 Symmetric Scales (Reference)

**Whole-Tone**: All whole steps; over augmented or 7#5 chords
C whole-tone: C D E F# G# Bb
- Use: Floating ambiguous passages (Debussy, anime score)

**Diminished (Whole-Half, WH)**: Eight-note scale
C dim WH: C D Eb F Gb Ab A B
- Use: Over diminished 7 chords

**Diminished (Half-Whole, HW)**: Eight-note scale
C dim HW: C Db D# E F# G A Bb
- Use: Over dominant b9 chords (jazz essential)

**Augmented Scale**: Augmented 2nd + half step alternating
C augmented: C D# E G Ab B
- Use: Over augmented chords, Coltrane-style cycles

## SECTION 2 — INTERVAL SYSTEM

### 2.1 Complete Interval Table (Ascending from Root)

| Semitones | Name | Quality | Character |
|-----------|------|---------|-----------|
| 0 | Perfect Unison (P1) | Perfect consonant | Same note |
| 1 | Minor 2nd (m2) | Strong dissonant | Half step, max tension |
| 2 | Major 2nd (M2) | Mild dissonant | Whole step |
| 3 | Minor 3rd (m3) | Imperfect consonant | Minor chord defining |
| 4 | Major 3rd (M3) | Imperfect consonant | Major chord defining |
| 5 | Perfect 4th (P4) | Consonant | Stable, sus chord |
| 6 | Tritone (TT) | Extreme dissonant | Maximum tension |
| 7 | Perfect 5th (P5) | Perfect consonant | Stable, power chord |
| 8 | Minor 6th (m6) | Imperfect consonant | Lyrical, soft |
| 9 | Major 6th (M6) | Imperfect consonant | Bright, pentatonic |
| 10 | Minor 7th (m7) | Mild dissonant | Bluesy, dominant |
| 11 | Major 7th (M7) | Strong dissonant | Lush jazz tension |
| 12 | Perfect Octave (P8) | Perfect consonant | Same note higher |

### 2.2 Quality Modifiers
- Augmented: raise major or perfect by 1 semitone
- Diminished: lower minor or perfect by 1 semitone
- Augmented 4th = Diminished 5th = Tritone (enharmonic)
- Augmented 6th = enharmonic minor 7th (used in special chords)

### 2.3 Tritone Pairs (For Tritone Substitution)
Each tritone splits the octave evenly. The six tritone pairs:
C↔F# / G↔Db / D↔Ab / A↔Eb / E↔Bb / B↔F

These pairs underlie tritone substitution: V7 chord may be
replaced by a dominant 7 a tritone away (e.g., G7 ↔ Db7).
Both share the same tritone (B and F), giving them the same
internal tension. See `03_HARMONY_ADVANCED.md` for application.

## SECTION 3 — CHORD SYSTEM

### 3.1 Triads

| Type | Formula | C example | Character |
|------|---------|-----------|-----------|
| Major | 1 3 5 | C E G | Bright, stable |
| Minor | 1 b3 5 | C Eb G | Sad, soft |
| Diminished | 1 b3 b5 | C Eb Gb | Tension, transient |
| Augmented | 1 3 #5 | C E G# | Floating, suspended |
| Sus2 | 1 2 5 | C D G | Open, neutral (no 3rd) |
| Sus4 | 1 4 5 | C F G | Suspended, wants resolution |
| Power chord | 1 5 | C G | Rock/metal aggression |

Symbols: maj or M = major / min, m, − = minor / dim, ° = diminished
/ aug, + = augmented / sus = suspended / 5 = power chord

### 3.2 Seventh Chords

| Type | Formula | C example | Symbol |
|------|---------|-----------|--------|
| Major 7 | 1 3 5 7 | C E G B | Cmaj7, CΔ7, CM7 |
| Dominant 7 | 1 3 5 b7 | C E G Bb | C7 |
| Minor 7 | 1 b3 5 b7 | C Eb G Bb | Cm7, C-7 |
| Minor-Major 7 | 1 b3 5 7 | C Eb G B | Cm(maj7), CmM7 |
| Half-Diminished 7 | 1 b3 b5 b7 | C Eb Gb Bb | Cm7b5, Cø7 |
| Diminished 7 | 1 b3 b5 bb7 | C Eb Gb A | Cdim7, C°7 |
| Augmented 7 | 1 3 #5 b7 | C E G# Bb | C7#5, C+7 |

Function summary:
- Major 7: Lush tonic in jazz, dreamy
- Dominant 7: Tension demanding resolution to I
- Minor 7: Soft minor, ii in ii-V-I, neo-soul foundation
- Minor-Major 7: "James Bond chord", cinematic minor with major 7
- Half-Diminished: ii in minor key ii-V-i
- Diminished 7: Symmetrical, leading-tone substitute
- Augmented 7: Altered dominant, fusion/jazz

### 3.3 Extensions (9, 11, 13)

**9th Chords** (add 9 above 7th)
- Cmaj9: C E G B D (1 3 5 7 9)
- C9: C E G Bb D (1 3 5 b7 9)
- Cm9: C Eb G Bb D (1 b3 5 b7 9)
- Function: Adds shimmer; defining color of neo-soul, modern R&B,
  modern jazz

**11th Chords** (add 11 above 9th)
- C11: C E G Bb D F
- Cm11: C Eb G Bb D F
- Caution: 11 with major 3 creates b9 dissonance (avoid in
  Cmaj11). Use #11 instead for bright Lydian-flavored major.

**13th Chords** (add 13 above 11th)
- C13: typically C E G Bb D A (omit 11)
- Cmaj13: C E G B D F# A (use #11 with major 3)
- Function: Full jazz color, big band, neo-soul richness

### 3.4 Altered Dominants (Reference)

For maximum tension before resolution:

- C7b9: C E G Bb Db
- C7#9: C E G Bb D# (Hendrix chord)
- C7#11: C E G Bb F# (Lydian dominant)
- C7b13: C E G Bb Ab
- C7alt: combines b9, #9, #11, b13

### 3.5 Sus and Slash Chords

**Sus chords** (suspended, no 3rd):
- Csus4: C F G (wants to resolve to C major)
- Csus2: C D G (open, neutral)
- C7sus4: C F G Bb (jazz, fusion, hip-hop)

**Slash chords** (specified bass note):
- C/E: C major triad with E in bass (1st inversion)
- C/G: C major with G in bass (2nd inversion, cadential 6-4)
- F/G: F triad over G bass (sus chord substitute)
- Am/F: A minor over F bass (Fmaj7 alternate voicing)

## SECTION 4 — DIATONIC HARMONY

### 4.1 Diatonic Triads in Major Keys

In any major key, the seven diatonic triads built from each
scale degree follow this pattern:

| Degree | Quality | Roman Numeral |
|--------|---------|---------------|
| 1 | Major | I |
| 2 | Minor | ii |
| 3 | Minor | iii |
| 4 | Major | IV |
| 5 | Major | V |
| 6 | Minor | vi |
| 7 | Diminished | vii° |

C major example: C, Dm, Em, F, G, Am, B°

### 4.2 Diatonic Seventh Chords in Major Keys

| Degree | Quality | Symbol |
|--------|---------|--------|
| 1 | Major 7 | Imaj7 |
| 2 | Minor 7 | iim7 |
| 3 | Minor 7 | iiim7 |
| 4 | Major 7 | IVmaj7 |
| 5 | Dominant 7 | V7 |
| 6 | Minor 7 | vim7 |
| 7 | Half-Diminished 7 | viim7b5 |

C major example:
Cmaj7, Dm7, Em7, Fmaj7, G7, Am7, Bm7b5

### 4.3 Diatonic Chords in Minor Keys (Natural Minor)

| Degree | Quality | Roman Numeral |
|--------|---------|---------------|
| 1 | Minor | i |
| 2 | Diminished | ii° |
| 3 | Major | III |
| 4 | Minor | iv |
| 5 | Minor (or V dominant from harmonic minor) | v / V |
| 6 | Major | VI |
| 7 | Major | VII |

A minor example: Am, B°, C, Dm, Em (or E), F, G

Note: In tonal minor music, the V chord is typically borrowed
from harmonic minor (E major in A minor) to provide a strong
leading tone for cadential resolution.

### 4.4 Chord Function Categories

Three primary harmonic functions in tonal music:

**Tonic function (T)** — stability, arrival, home
- Chords: I, iii, vi (in major); i, III, VI (in minor)
- Substitutes: iii and vi can substitute for I in deceptive
  contexts or for tonic prolongation

**Subdominant / Predominant function (S)** — motion away from
tonic, preparation for dominant
- Chords: ii, IV (major); iiø, iv, bVI (minor)
- ii is the most common pre-dominant in jazz

**Dominant function (D)** — maximum tension, demands resolution
to tonic
- Chords: V, vii° (and extensions V7, V9, V13, V7alt)

Standard functional path: T → S → D → T

Common functional progressions:
- I-IV-V-I (T-S-D-T)
- I-vi-ii-V-I (T-T-S-D-T, "circle progression")
- I-V-vi-IV ("axis progression", T-D-T-S)

## SECTION 5 — CADENCES

A cadence is a harmonic conclusion to a phrase. Cadences vary
in conclusiveness from full closure to open-ended.

### 5.1 Authentic Cadences

**Perfect Authentic Cadence (PAC)** — strongest closure
- Formula: V → I (or V7 → I)
- Voicing: Both chords in root position, tonic root in soprano
- Use: End of pieces, end of major sections
- Strength: Maximum

**Imperfect Authentic Cadence (IAC)** — strong but lighter
- Formula: V → I with chord inversions or 3rd/5th in soprano
- Use: Antecedent endings, internal phrase ends
- Strength: Strong, less conclusive than PAC

### 5.2 Half Cadence
- Formula: ends on V (any chord → V)
- Effect: Open, unresolved, "question" feel
- Use: End of antecedent phrase, midpoint pauses
- Variant: Phrygian Half (iv6 → V) — minor key bittersweet color

### 5.3 Plagal Cadence ("Amen Cadence")
- Formula: IV → I
- Use: Soft endings, hymn endings, extension after PAC
- Variants: Minor plagal (iv → I), double plagal (bVII → IV → I)

### 5.4 Deceptive Cadence
- Formula: V → vi (instead of expected I)
- Minor variant: V → bVI
- Use: Surprise, delay closure, set up new section
- Effect: Listener expects I, receives vi/bVI — emotional tension

### 5.5 Cadence Strength Ranking (Most to Least Conclusive)

1. PAC — Perfect Authentic
2. IAC — Imperfect Authentic, root position
3. IAC — Imperfect Authentic, inverted
4. Plagal Cadence
5. Phrygian Half (minor)
6. Half Cadence
7. Deceptive Cadence
8. Elision (no cadence, run-on phrase)

### 5.6 Jazz Cadences (Reference)

- ii-V-I (major): Dm7-G7-Cmaj7 (canonical jazz cadence)
- ii-V-i (minor): Dm7b5-G7b9-Cm(maj7)
- ii-V-I with tritone sub: Dm7-Db7-Cmaj7
- Backdoor cadence: iv-bVII7-I (Fm7-Bb7-Cmaj7)
- Cycle cadence: iii-vi-ii-V-I

## SECTION 6 — VOICE LEADING PRINCIPLES

Voice leading governs how individual voice lines move between
chords. Good voice leading produces smoothness, independence,
and clarity. Poor voice leading produces awkward jumps, parallel
errors, or clouded textures.

### 6.1 Four Fundamental Principles

**Principle 1: Common tone retention**
When two adjacent chords share a note, keep that note in the
same voice. Example: C → Am shares E. Keep E stationary.

**Principle 2: Stepwise motion preferred**
When voices must move, prefer movement by step (whole or half)
over leaps. Voices that move by step sound smooth.

**Principle 3: Contrary motion between outer voices**
When the bass moves up, the soprano should generally move down,
and vice versa. Contrary motion produces independence between
voices.

**Principle 4: Resolve tendency tones**
- Leading tone (7th scale degree) resolves up to tonic
- Chordal 7th of dominant resolves down by step
- Suspended 4 resolves down to 3
- Diminished 5th resolves inward
- Augmented 4th resolves outward

### 6.2 Common Voice Leading Errors to Avoid

**Parallel fifths**: Two voices move from one perfect 5th to
another perfect 5th in the same direction. Considered poor
voice leading in classical and most pop arrangement contexts.

**Parallel octaves**: Two voices move from octave to octave in
the same direction. Reduces independence to seven voices acting
as one.

**Direct (hidden) fifths or octaves**: Outer voices arrive at a
perfect 5th or octave by similar motion. Less serious than
parallels but still considered weak in formal contexts.

**Voice crossing**: Lower voice moving above the voice
immediately above it. Acceptable occasionally but generally
avoided for clarity.

**Voice overlap**: Adjacent voice arrives at a pitch that one
voice was just on. Mildly weakens voice independence.

### 6.3 Practical Voice Leading for Pop and Songwriting

In contemporary pop production, strict classical voice leading
rules relax. Parallel fifths in power chord rock are stylistic.
Parallel motion in EDM stacks is intentional. The principles
that remain critical:

- Bass line should be melodic, not just root motion
- Common tones should be preserved across chord changes
- Inner voices should move by step where possible
- Avoid all voices leaping in the same direction simultaneously
- Resolve dissonances and tendency tones consciously

## SECTION 8 — CIRCLE OF FIFTHS

### 8.1 Major Key Relationships

Clockwise (sharps increase):
C(0) → G(1#) → D(2#) → A(3#) → E(4#) → B(5#) → F#(6#) → C#(7#)

Counterclockwise (flats increase):
C(0) → F(1b) → Bb(2b) → Eb(3b) → Ab(4b) → Db(5b) → Gb(6b) → Cb(7b)

### 8.2 Relative Minor Relationships

Each major key has a relative minor a minor 3rd below:

| Major | Relative Minor |
|-------|---------------|
| C | Am |
| G | Em |
| D | Bm |
| A | F#m |
| E | C#m |
| B | G#m |
| F | Dm |
| Bb | Gm |
| Eb | Cm |
| Ab | Fm |

### 8.3 Uses of the Circle

- Identifying closely related keys (adjacent on circle = 1
  accidental difference, smooth modulation)
- Building ii-V-I cycles through multiple keys
- Understanding classical key relationships
- Predicting common modulations in pop and jazz

## SECTION 10 — CHORD VOICING ESSENTIALS

### 10.1 Open vs Closed Voicing

**Closed voicing**: All chord tones within one octave
- Tight, focused sound
- Used in vocal harmony, brass section block harmony

**Open voicing**: Chord tones spread across more than one octave
- Open, spacious sound
- Used in piano accompaniment, orchestral arrangement

### 10.2 Inversions

A chord may be voiced with any chord tone in the bass:

- Root position: root in bass (C-E-G with C in bass)
- 1st inversion: 3rd in bass (E-G-C with E in bass) → C/E
- 2nd inversion: 5th in bass (G-C-E with G in bass) → C/G
- 3rd inversion (7th chords): 7th in bass (B-C-E-G with B in bass)
  → Cmaj7/B

Inversions enable smoother bass lines and more sophisticated
voice leading.

### 10.3 Rootless Voicings (Jazz Essential)

When a bassist plays the root, the harmony player can omit it:

- Cmaj7 rootless A: E G B D (3-5-7-9, omits root and 5)
- Cmaj7 rootless B: B D E G (7-9-3-5)
- G7 rootless A: B F A E (3-b7-9-13)
- G7 rootless B: F A B E (b7-9-3-13)
- G7 altered rootless: B F Ab Eb (3-b7-b9-b13)

These voicings are foundational to modern jazz piano comping.

### 10.4 Shell Voicings (Minimal Jazz)

The most economical voicing: root + 3 + 7, omits the 5.

- Cmaj7 shell: C E B
- Dm7 shell: D F C
- G7 shell: G B F

Used in solo piano left hand, big band brass section.

### 10.5 Drop-2 and Drop-3 Voicings

**Drop-2**: Take the second voice from the top of close-position
voicing, drop it down an octave.
- Cmaj7 close: C-E-G-B
- Cmaj7 drop-2: G-C-E-B

**Drop-3**: Drop the third voice from the top.
- Cmaj7 drop-3: E-G-C-B

These voicings are foundational to 4-string guitar comping and
big band saxophone soli writing.

### 10.6 Quartal Voicings (Modal Jazz)

Stack perfect 4ths instead of thirds:

- C quartal 3-note: C F Bb
- C quartal 4-note: C F Bb Eb

Use: Modal jazz (McCoy Tyner), spiritual jazz, modern fusion,
ambient harmony.

## SECTION 11 — APPLICATION GUIDE

### 11.1 Choosing Scale and Key

Decision criteria:
- Singer's vocal range and tessitura
- Emotional target (major bright / minor sad / modal specific)
- Genre conventions (jazz favors flat keys, guitar favors sharp keys)
- Modulation plans (does the key change later?)
- Reference tracks in similar style

Common pop key zones:
- Female lead pop: D, E, F, G, A, Bb major or relative minors
- Male lead pop: C, D, E, F, G major or relative minors
- Guitar-driven: D, E, G, A major (open chord friendly)
- Piano-driven: C, F, Bb, Eb major (flat-key piano)

### 11.2 Choosing Chord Vocabulary Level

The system recognizes four chord vocabulary levels:

**Level 0 — Plain triads**
Example: C G Am F
Use: Folk, country, punk, garage rock

**Level 1 — Diatonic 7ths**
Example: Cmaj7 G7 Am7 Fmaj7
Use: Pop ballad, indie, soft rock, mainstream production

**Level 2 — 9th extensions**
Example: Cmaj9 G9 Am9 Fmaj9
Use: Neo-soul, modern R&B, sophisticated pop

**Level 3 — Full jazz extensions**
Example: Cmaj13 G13 Am11 Fmaj7#11
Use: Jazz, fusion, complex arrangement

Match the vocabulary level to the genre and emotional target.
Mixing levels within a song is acceptable; mixing levels within
a single section often sounds inconsistent.

### 11.3 Common Pitfalls

- Choosing a key that pushes vocalist past comfortable range
- Using overly complex harmony in genres that demand simplicity
- Ignoring voice leading in production where parts will be heard
- Modulating without a pivot or smooth voice leading
- Stacking chord extensions that create unintended dissonance
  (e.g., 11 with major 3 producing b9 clash)
- Defaulting to I-V-vi-IV without considering alternatives

### §UE-1. SJY Harmony Cheat Sheets (5종 통합)

#### §UE-1.1 Functional Harmony Cheat Sheet

```
Tonic (T): I, vi (iii)
Predominant (PD): ii, IV (vi, iii)
Dominant (D): V, vii°
Tonic Substitute: vi, iii (deceptive)

Standard Progressions:
- T-PD-D-T (I-ii-V-I)
- T-D-T (I-V-I)
- T-T-PD-D (I-vi-IV-V — 50s progression)
- T-D-PD-T (I-V-IV-I — Mixolydian)

Modal:
- Dorian: i-IV (major IV in minor key)
- Mixolydian: I-bVII (lowered 7th)
- Lydian: I-II (major II)
- Phrygian: i-bII (Spanish gypsy)
```

#### §UE-1.2 Voice Leading Cheat Sheet

```
Smooth Voice Leading 원칙:
1. Common tone retention (공통음 유지)
2. Stepwise motion (계단 진행)
3. Contrary motion preferred over parallel
4. No parallel 5ths / 8ves between voices
5. Resolve leading tone (7→1)
6. Resolve 7th of chord (down)

Voice spacing:
- Open voicing: > 1 octave
- Close voicing: < 1 octave
- Drop 2 voicing: 2nd voice down 1 octave
```

#### §UE-1.3 Cadences Cheat Sheet

```
Authentic Cadence (PAC):
  V-I (strong, conclusive)
  
Half Cadence (HC):
  Anything → V (open, unresolved)
  
Plagal Cadence:
  IV-I (amen cadence)
  
Deceptive Cadence:
  V-vi (surprise)
  
Phrygian Half Cadence:
  iv6-V (in minor — dramatic)

Suno 어법:
- Verse 끝: HC 또는 Deceptive (continue)
- Pre-Chorus 끝: HC 강한 (build to chorus)
- Chorus 끝: PAC 강한 (resolution)
- Bridge 끝: HC 또는 V/I sustained (return to chorus)
```

#### §UE-1.4 Secondary Dominants Cheat Sheet

```
V/V → V (서브도미넌트 to 도미넌트)
V/IV → IV (밝은 turnaround)
V/vi → vi (slow soulful)
V/ii → ii (chromatic descent prep)

C major 예:
V/V = D7 → G7 → C
V/IV = C7 → F → C
V/vi = E7 → Am → C
V/ii = A7 → Dm → C

Tritone Sub:
V/V tritone sub = Ab7 → G7 (대체)
```

#### §UE-1.5 Modal Mixture Cheat Sheet

```
Borrowed from parallel minor (in major key):
- iv (minor IV — "찡함")
- bVI (lowered 6)
- bVII (lowered 7 — mixolydian)
- bIII (lowered 3 — chromatic mediant)

C major 예:
- iv = Fm (instead of F)
- bVI = Ab (instead of Am)
- bVII = Bb (instead of Bm)
- bIII = Eb (instead of Em)

Suno 어법:
- Verse → Pre-Chorus: borrowed iv 자리에 박음
- Bridge: bVI 또는 bVII 활용
- Chromatic mediant: 키 chord progression의 색감 변화
```
