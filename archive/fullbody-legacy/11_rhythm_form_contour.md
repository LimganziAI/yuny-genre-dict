## SECTION 1 — TIME SIGNATURES

### 1.1 Common Time Signatures

**4/4 — Common Time**
- Four quarter-note beats per measure
- Default for pop, rock, jazz, hip-hop, electronic, and most
  contemporary genres
- Approximately 90% of contemporary popular music

**3/4 — Waltz Time**
- Three quarter-note beats per measure
- Default for waltzes, country waltzes, some classical
- Strong-weak-weak feel
- Examples: "My Favorite Things", "Norwegian Wood"

**6/8 — Compound Duple**
- Six eighth-note beats per measure, felt as two groups of three
- Default for R&B shuffle, gospel, ballad, Celtic jig
- Lilting, swung feel inherent in time signature
- Examples: "We Are the Champions", "Nothing Else Matters"

**12/8 — Compound Quadruple**
- Twelve eighth-note beats per measure, felt as four groups of three
- Default for blues shuffle, doo-wop, gospel ballad
- Heavy triplet feel
- Examples: "I Can't Make You Love Me", classic blues ballads

### 1.2 Less Common Time Signatures

**5/4 — Quintuple Time**
- Five beats per measure
- Grouping: typically (3+2) or (2+3)
- Examples: Dave Brubeck "Take Five" (3+3+2+2 hybrid),
  Lalo Schifrin "Mission: Impossible" (3+2)

**7/8 — Septuple Time**
- Seven eighth-notes per measure
- Common groupings: (2+2+3), (3+2+2), (2+3+2)
- Use: Eastern European folk, prog rock, prog metal, math rock
- Examples: Pink Floyd "Money" (7/4), Tool "Schism" (5+7),
  Bulgarian folk traditions

**9/8 — Compound Triple**
- Nine eighth-notes, typically (3+3+3) or (2+2+2+3)
- Use: Some jazz waltzes, Greek folk, Irish slip jig
- Examples: Dave Brubeck "Blue Rondo à la Turk"

**11/8 — Asymmetric Eleven**
- Eleven eighth-notes, common groupings (2+2+2+2+3) or (3+3+3+2)
- Rare in pop, more common in prog, math rock, world music
- Examples: Genesis "Apocalypse in 9/8" sections, Don Ellis works

### 1.3 Mixed Meter

Some compositions change time signatures throughout, often
within a single song:
- Verse in 4/4, bridge in 3/4 (or vice versa)
- Math rock with bar-by-bar shifts
- Prog rock multi-section pieces
- Examples: Radiohead "Pyramid Song" (mixed), King Crimson works

### 1.4 Implied Meter (Half-Time and Double-Time Feel)

A song's notated BPM may differ from its perceptual feel:

**Half-time feel**: Snare lands on beat 3 only (instead of 2 and 4),
making 140 BPM perceptually feel like 70 BPM
- Genres: Trap, drill, modern R&B chorus, future-bass drop
- Effect: Heaviness with high-energy hi-hats on top

**Double-time feel**: Snare lands on every offbeat, making
70 BPM perceptually feel like 140 BPM
- Genres: Punk, hardcore, fast bluegrass, drum and bass
- Effect: Urgency without changing tempo

## SECTION 2 — RHYTHMIC FOUNDATIONS

### 2.1 Beat, Pulse, Subdivision

**Beat**: The basic pulse unit (typically the quarter note in 4/4)
**Pulse**: The grouped feel of beats (typically 4-beat measures)
**Subdivision**: Smaller units within the beat
- 8th notes: 2 per beat
- 16th notes: 4 per beat
- 32nd notes: 8 per beat
- Triplet 8ths: 3 per beat
- Triplet 16ths: 6 per beat

### 2.2 Strong and Weak Beats

In 4/4:
- Beat 1: Strongest
- Beat 3: Second strongest
- Beats 2 and 4: Weak (but receive backbeat in pop/rock)
- "and" of any beat: Weakest

This hierarchy informs:
- Where chord changes typically land (strong beats)
- Where ghost notes and syncopation work (weak beats)
- Where lyric stress should fall (strong beats)

### 2.3 Backbeat

The defining feature of pop, rock, R&B, and most contemporary
popular music:
- Snare drum on beats 2 and 4
- Kick drum on beats 1 and 3 (basic) or syncopated (modern)
- Hi-hat 8ths or 16ths filling the subdivision

The backbeat reverses classical strong-weak hierarchy by
emphasizing the "weak" beats 2 and 4. This is what makes
backbeat-driven music feel different from waltz or march music.

### 2.4 Syncopation

Syncopation places accents on weak beats or off-beats. Common
syncopation devices:

**Anticipation**: Note arrives before the beat (push)
- "And of 4" anticipating beat 1 of next measure
- Common in funk and Latin

**Delayed resolution**: Note arrives after the beat (lay back)
- Common in hip-hop, neo-soul, jazz

**Tied notes across bar lines**: Note crosses the bar line
- Creates rhythmic flow across measures

**Rest on strong beat**: Silence where downbeat is expected
- Used in funk, EDM drops, dramatic moments

**Hemiola**: 3-against-2 polyrhythm
- Three notes occupy the space of two beats
- Or two notes occupy the space of three beats

**Metric displacement**: Pattern shifted by 1-2 beats from
expected position
- Used in modern jazz, fusion, and rhythmic displacement
  harmony

## SECTION 3 — GROOVE FORMULAS PER GENRE

Each genre has signature rhythmic patterns that define its
feel. These are foundational templates; variations are common.

### 3.1 Pop / Rock Standard
- Kick: beats 1 and 3
- Snare: beats 2 and 4
- Hi-hat: 8th notes
- Bass: root-fifth pattern in verses, eighth-note drive in
  choruses
- Ghost notes: minimal

### 3.2 Funk
- Kick: beat 1 and offbeat (typically "and of 2" or "and of 3")
- Snare: beats 2 and 4 with ghost notes between
- Hi-hat: 16th notes with accent variation
- Bass: slap, fingered melodic lines, syncopated 16ths
- Ghost notes: extensive on snare and bass
- Genre signature: tight pocket, James Brown / Sly Stone lineage

### 3.3 Reggae (One-Drop)
- Kick: beat 3 only
- Snare: beat 3 (paired with kick)
- Hi-hat: off-beat upstroke pattern
- Skank guitar: off-beats (and-of-1, and-of-2, and-of-3, and-of-4)
- Bass: heavy melodic syncopated lines, occupies space drums leave
- Genre signature: empty beats 1 and 2 are foundational

### 3.4 Bossa Nova
- Brushed snare or rim click on beats 2 and 4
- Bass: syncopated 2/4 pattern with chord stabs on 1 and "and of 3"
- João Gilberto guitar pattern: bass note on 1, syncopated upper
  chord stabs across the bar
- Tamborim 16th-note ostinato

### 3.5 Hip-Hop (Boom-Bap)
- Kick: beat 1 and "a of 3" (slightly delayed)
- Snare: beats 2 and 4 (rim or full snare)
- Hi-hat: shuffled 8ths with drag
- Drummer plays slightly behind grid (lay-back)
- MPC humanize feel — quantization 80-90% rather than 100%

### 3.6 Trap
- Kick: 808 sustained on syncopated downbeats
- Snare: beat 3 (half-time feel)
- Hi-hat: 16th notes with triplet rolls and 32nd skitters
- Note: Beat 3 placement creates the half-time perception even
  at 140 BPM tempo

### 3.7 UK Drill
- Sliding 808: pitched sub-bass that glides between chord tones
  (this IS the harmonic motion)
- Snare: beat 3 only, hard hit
- Hi-hat: off-grid 16th patterns with triplet rolls
- Pattern signature: "dn-dn-dn" sliding bass Y-shape glides

### 3.8 UK Garage / 2-Step
- Kick: beats 1 and 3 with displaced "and-of" hits
- Snare: beat 2 and "and of 3" (skipped pattern)
- Hi-hat: syncopated, off-grid placement
- Pattern signature: NOT four-on-floor (this is the defining
  feature distinguishing 2-step from house)
- Shaker or rim-click filling 8th-note grid
- Sub-bass on syncopated 16ths

### 3.9 House / Techno (Four-on-Floor)
- Kick: every beat (1, 2, 3, 4)
- Open hi-hat: off-beats (and-of-1, and-of-2, and-of-3, and-of-4)
- Clap or snare: beats 2 and 4
- Shaker or rim: 16th note ostinato

### 3.10 Drum and Bass / Jungle
- Amen Break foundation: chopped breakbeat from "Amen, Brother"
- Snare: beats 2 and 4 with ghost-note 16th hits between
- Kick: syncopated, not four-on-floor
- Hi-hat: 16th skitters with breakbeat humanization
- Tempo: 165-180 BPM

### 3.11 Jazz Swing
- Ride cymbal: "ding-da-ding" pattern with triplet feel
- Hi-hat: foot pedal on beats 2 and 4
- Bass: walking quarters
- Snare: comping accents (varied placement)
- Swing ratio: typically 60:40 to 67:33 (triplet-derived)

### 3.12 Latin / Salsa
- Clave: 3-2 or 2-3 pattern (the foundation of all Latin music)
- Conga, bongo, timbale layered polyrhythmically
- Cowbell, güiro, maracas filling the top
- Bass: tumbao pattern (syncopated, hits "and of 2" and "4")

### 3.13 Afrobeats
- Clave-derived 3-2 son under straight kick
- Shaker on every 8th
- Rim click on syncopated downbeats
- Layered percussion creating dense polyrhythmic top

### 3.14 Amapiano
- Log drum: pitched melodic-bass hybrid playing syncopated
  16th patterns (genre signature)
- Deep house-derived kick on 1
- Layered shakers and claps
- Polyrhythmic top end

### 3.15 Country (Train Beat)
- Kick: beats 1 and 3
- Snare: rapid 8th-note cross-stick or brushed
- Hi-hat: 8ths or 16ths
- Bass: alternating root-fifth (Carter family pattern)

## SECTION 4 — MICROTIMING AND POCKET

### 4.1 What Is Microtiming?

Microtiming refers to deliberate timing deviations of 10-50
milliseconds from the metronomic grid. These deviations create
"pocket" — the groove that makes listeners involuntarily move.

**Categories of microtiming**:

**Ahead of beat (push)**:
- 10-20ms before grid
- Effect: Energy, urgency, forward motion
- Genres: Rock push, funk push (some funk drummers)

**On beat (grid)**:
- 0ms deviation, exactly on click
- Effect: Mechanical, programmed feel
- Genres: EDM, modern pop, programmed productions

**Behind the beat (lay-back)**:
- 10-30ms after grid
- Effect: Relaxed, cool, lazy feel
- Genres: Hip-hop, neo-soul, jazz, R&B

### 4.2 Swing Ratios

The relationship between consecutive 8th notes determines swing
character:

| Ratio | Description | Genre |
|-------|-------------|-------|
| 50:50 | Straight 8ths | EDM, modern pop, mechanical |
| 55:45 | Slight swing | Pop with feel |
| 60:40 | Medium swing | Hip-hop shuffle, R&B |
| 67:33 | Triplet swing | Jazz swing |
| 70:30 | Heavy swing | Blues shuffle, slow blues |

Modern producers can dial swing in DAW (Ableton "Groove Pool",
Logic "Quantize Strength", FL Studio "Swing").

### 4.3 Ghost Notes

**Definition**: Quiet drum hits filling the 16th note grid,
typically at 25-40% of the velocity of accent hits.

**Function**:
- Fill rhythmic gaps without competing with vocal
- Create groove density that listeners feel rather than consciously
  hear
- Snare drum ghost notes are the foundation of funk, R&B, and
  neo-soul drum feel

**Standard placement** (16th grid in 4/4):
- Beats 2 and 4: full snare (accent)
- 16th positions between: ghost notes at low velocity
- Pattern: 1-e-and-a / 2-e-and-a / 3-e-and-a / 4-e-and-a
  with accents on 2 and 4, ghosts elsewhere

### 4.4 Pocket Construction

Pocket emerges from:

1. **Velocity variation**: Not all hits at 100%; humanization
   creates organic feel
2. **Microtiming consistency**: A drummer playing consistently
   behind the beat creates predictable lay-back
3. **Bass-drum lock**: Bass and kick locked together (or bass
   playing in the gaps the kick leaves)
4. **Subdivision density**: Filling the 16th grid with ghost
   notes, hat patterns, percussion

### 4.5 Quantization Levels

When programming drums in DAW:

| Level | Effect | Use |
|-------|--------|-----|
| 100% 16ths | Mechanical, perfect grid | EDM, trap, modern pop |
| 80-90% 16ths | Tight but human | Pop with feel |
| 100% 16th triplets | Mechanical swing | Modern hip-hop programmed |
| 50% | Light correction, jazz feel | Jazz, fusion |
| Off (no quantize) | Pure performance | Live recording, jazz, folk |

Match quantization level to genre. Over-quantizing folk or jazz
removes the organic feel that defines those genres.

## SECTION 5 — POLYRHYTHM AND POLYMETER

### 5.1 Polyrhythm Definition

**Polyrhythm**: Two or more different rhythmic patterns played
simultaneously, sharing a common pulse but dividing the beat
differently.

**Common polyrhythms**:

**3-against-2 (Hemiola)**
- Three notes occupy the same time as two beats
- The most common polyrhythm; foundational to African and Latin
  music
- Use: Funk, R&B fills, jazz, world music

**4-against-3**
- Four notes occupy the space of three beats
- More complex; appears in advanced jazz, prog metal

**5-against-4**
- Five notes occupy the space of four beats
- Advanced jazz, prog metal, modern fusion

**7-against-8**
- Math rock (Polyphia, Animals as Leaders)
- Highly advanced; requires precise calculation

### 5.2 Polymeter Definition

**Polymeter**: Two or more different time signatures occurring
simultaneously, sharing the same pulse but cycling at different
rates.

**Example polymeter**:
- Drums in 4/4
- Bass riff in 7/4
- The patterns sync up after 28 beats (LCM of 4 and 7)

**Use**: Math rock, prog metal, Tool, Meshuggah, Polyphia.
Creates a trance-like effect as the patterns gradually shift
phase before realigning.

### 5.3 Polyrhythm vs Polymeter

The distinction:
- Polyrhythm: same total length (beats), different subdivision
- Polymeter: different total length (beat counts), same pulse

Both can coexist; advanced math rock often uses both
simultaneously.

### 5.4 Application Without Confusion

To use polyrhythm without losing the listener:

1. Lock one element to the main 4/4 grid (drums, typically)
2. Apply polyrhythm to a top layer (shaker, secondary
   percussion, melodic line)
3. Pan the polyrhythmic element off-center to separate it
   sonically from the main groove
4. Keep polyrhythmic volume below the main groove (6-9 dB lower)
5. Limit to one or two polyrhythmic layers; three or more
   creates incomprehensibility

## SECTION 6 — SONG FORMS

### 6.1 Verse-Chorus Form (VCVCBC)

The dominant form in contemporary popular music.

**Standard structure**:

Intro → Verse 1 → Chorus → Verse 2 → Chorus → Bridge → Chorus → Outro

**With pre-chorus**:
Intro → Verse 1 → Pre-Chorus → Chorus → Verse 2 → Pre-Chorus → Chorus → Bridge → Chorus → Outro

**Section function**:
- Verse: Sets up the scene, tells the story, lower energy
- Pre-Chorus: Builds tension toward chorus, climbing energy
- Chorus: Emotional payoff, memorable hook, highest energy
- Bridge: Twist, contrast, new perspective
- Outro: Resolution or fade

### 6.2 32-Bar AABA Form

Classic Tin Pan Alley and jazz standard form.

**Structure**: A(8) - A(8) - B(8) - A(8)
- A section: Main melody, repeated with possible variation
- B section: "Bridge" or "middle eight," contrasting melody
  and often different key

**Used in**:
- Jazz standards (most pre-1960s)
- Beatles early period ("Yesterday", "Something")
- Tin Pan Alley songs (Gershwin, Cole Porter)
- Some modern revival pop

### 6.3 K-Pop Multi-Section Form

K-pop developed extended structures with more sections than
Western pop.

**Standard 4th-generation K-pop structure**:
Intro (8) → Verse 1 (8-16) → Pre-Chorus (4-8) → Chorus (8) → Post-Chorus (4-8) → Verse 2 (8-16) → Pre-Chorus (4-8) → Chorus (8) → Rap Verse (8) → Bridge (8) → Dance Break (8-16, often instrumental) → Final Chorus (8, often modulated up) → Outro (4-8)

**Distinctive K-pop sections**:
- **Post-chorus**: A "second hook" after the main chorus,
  often with different melody, sometimes wordless ("yeah"
  vocables or nonsense syllables)
- **Rap verse**: Dedicated rap section, often by member
  designated as rapper
- **Dance break**: Mostly instrumental section for choreography,
  sometimes with single vocal phrase
- **Modulated final chorus**: Half-step or whole-step lift
  before the final chorus, near-mandatory in 2010s+ K-pop

### 6.4 Hip-Hop Song Form

Hip-hop typically follows a verse-hook architecture with longer
verses and shorter hooks than pop.

**Standard hip-hop structure**:
Intro (4-8) → Hook (8) → Verse 1 (16) → Hook (8) → Verse 2 (16) → Hook (8) → Bridge (4-8) → Hook (8) → Outro (4)

**Bar count conventions**:
- Verses: 16 bars (the standard "16-bar verse")
- Hooks/Choruses: 8 bars
- Some songs use 12-bar verses for shorter form
- Trap and drill often shorten to 8-bar verses

### 6.5 EDM / DJ Form

Designed for DJ mixing and dance floor function.

**Standard EDM structure**:
Intro (32) → Buildup (16) → Drop (32) → Breakdown (16) → Buildup (16) → Drop (32) → Outro (32)

**Section function**:
- Intro: DJ-friendly mixing entry, 32 bars of beat with minimal
  melodic content
- Buildup: Risers, snare rolls, increasing density, anticipation
- Drop: The main melodic/rhythmic payoff, full energy
- Breakdown: Drums removed, melodic and atmospheric content
- Outro: DJ-friendly mixing exit, beat with reduced content

**Subgenre variants**:
- Big-room: emphasizes drop impact, 32-bar drops
- Future bass: replaces traditional drums with pitched chord
  stabs in drop
- Progressive: longer build-ups, more melodic emphasis
- Trance: extended forms (8+ minutes), gradual evolution

### 6.6 12-Bar Blues Form

Foundational form for blues and many roots-rock genres.

**Standard 12-bar blues in C**:
| C7 | C7 | C7 | C7 | | F7 | F7 | C7 | C7 | | G7 | F7 | C7 | G7 |

**Quick-change variation** (replaces bar 2 with IV):
| C7 | F7 | C7 | C7 | | F7 | F7 | C7 | C7 | | G7 | F7 | C7 | G7 |

Used in blues, rock and roll, country blues, jazz blues, and
extensively as a song or solo vehicle.

### 6.7 Through-Composed and Strophic Forms

**Through-composed**: No section repeats, continuous new material
- Use: Art song (Schubert), opera, prog rock epics
- Examples: Queen "Bohemian Rhapsody"

**Strophic**: Same music for each stanza of text
- Use: Folk songs, hymns, traditional ballads
- Examples: "Amazing Grace", "Auld Lang Syne"

### 6.8 Hybrid and Modular Forms

Modern productions often combine elements:
- Hip-hop verse with K-pop chorus
- EDM drop with traditional verse-chorus surrounding
- AABA with extended outro vamp
- Verse-chorus with dance break inserted

The form serves the song, not vice versa. Choose form based on
emotional arc, energy curve, and listener attention design.

## SECTION 7 — TRANSITIONS AND DYNAMIC ARC

### 7.1 The Energy Curve

Every effective song has a managed energy curve from start to
end. Common curves:

**Standard pop curve**:
- Intro: 30-40% energy
- Verse 1: 40-50%
- Pre-Chorus: 60-70%
- Chorus: 80-90%
- Verse 2: 50-60% (slightly higher than V1)
- Pre-Chorus 2: 70-80%
- Chorus 2: 90%
- Bridge: 40-60% (variable, often dropped before final climax)
- Final Chorus: 100% (often modulated)
- Outro: 40-50% (decay)

**Slow build curve** (ballad):
- Gradual increase across entire song
- Final chorus reaches catharsis
- No major dropdowns

**Drop curve** (EDM):
- Buildup ascends to 100%
- Drop sustains 100% through main melodic statement
- Breakdown drops to 30-40%
- Second buildup re-ascends
- Final drop maintains 100%

### 7.2 Transition Devices

Transitions connect sections smoothly or with deliberate impact.

**Build-up devices**:
- Add layers progressively (instrument count increases)
- Filter sweep (lowpass opens up)
- Riser (synth sweep ascending)
- Snare roll
- Drum subdivision tightens (8ths to 16ths to 32nds)
- Vocal climbs in register
- Sub-bass rises in volume

**Drop devices**:
- Cymbal crash on downbeat of new section
- Sub-bass slam
- Layered harmony entry
- Drum kit re-entry after silence

**Breakdown devices**:
- Drums removed
- Strip to single instrument or vocal
- Harmonic stasis (single chord or pedal)
- Reverb tails extending

**Silence as device**:
- Brief absolute silence (0.3-0.7 seconds) before chorus or
  drop creates massive impact
- All instruments and reverb tails cut simultaneously
- Used by Adele "Rolling in the Deep" pre-chorus, gospel
  music traditionally, modern festival EDM

### 7.3 Section-to-Section Transition Patterns

**Verse to Pre-Chorus**:
- Add one instrument layer
- Increase drum subdivision
- Vocal melody climbs slightly

**Pre-Chorus to Chorus**:
- All build devices stack and release on chorus downbeat
- Cymbal crash
- Layered harmony enters
- Sub-bass slams

**Chorus to Verse**:
- Density drops by 30-50%
- Lead vocal layers reduce to single track
- Bass simplifies pattern
- Reverb tail maintains continuity

**Chorus to Bridge**:
- Often a dramatic shift in arrangement or key
- Strip to fewer elements for contrast
- New instrument or texture introduced

**Bridge to Final Chorus**:
- Build-up similar to pre-chorus into chorus
- Often paired with key modulation (half-step lift)
- Maximum arrangement density

### 7.4 Bar Count Targeting (Suno-Specific)

When generating with Suno, explicit bar counts in section tags
help control section length:

[Intro 4] [Verse 1 8] [Pre-Chorus 4] [Chorus 8] [Verse 2 8] [Pre-Chorus 4] [Chorus 8] [Bridge 8] [Final Chorus 8] [Outro 4]

These are targets, not strict rules — Suno may approximate
±20%. Bar counts prevent the engine from compressing or
expanding sections unpredictably.

## SECTION 8 — MOTIF DEVELOPMENT

A motif is a short melodic-rhythmic idea (2-8 notes, typically
1-2 bars) that serves as compositional building material.

### 8.1 Why Motif Development

Motifs allow a song to feel unified despite varying material:
- Listener recognizes the motif when it returns
- Variations create interest while preserving identity
- Long-form pieces (classical, prog rock, film score) rely on
  motif recurrence for coherence

### 8.2 Twelve Motif Development Techniques

**1. Exact repetition**
- Same motif, same pitch and rhythm
- Creates emphasis and memorability

**2. Sequence**
- Motif transposed to different pitch level
- Tonal sequence: stays in key
- Real sequence: exact transposition (changes key)
- Modulating sequence: gradually moves toward new key

**3. Inversion**
- Flip the intervals (ascending becomes descending)
- Strict inversion: exact interval flip
- Tonal inversion: stay in key while flipping contour

**4. Retrograde**
- Reverse the order of notes
- More common in classical and serial music

**5. Retrograde-inversion**
- Reverse and flip
- Highly transformative; often unrecognizable as the original

**6. Augmentation**
- Multiply note durations (typically by 2)
- Quarter notes become half notes; effect: slower, more
  monumental

**7. Diminution**
- Divide note durations (typically by 2)
- Half notes become quarter notes; effect: faster, more urgent

**8. Fragmentation**
- Use only part of the motif
- Often the most distinctive 2-3 notes
- Allows the motif to function in tighter spaces

**9. Extension**
- Add notes to the motif
- Continues the melodic line beyond original

**10. Liquidation**
- Gradually dissolve the motif
- Reduces it to its most essential element, then to nothing
- Used as a transition or ending technique

**11. Modal transformation**
- Change the mode of the motif
- Major motif played in minor (or vice versa)
- Same intervals, different scale context

**12. Harmonic re-contextualization**
- Same motif, new harmony beneath it
- Listener perceives the motif differently due to new
  harmonic implications

### 8.3 Motif Use in Pop vs Classical

**Classical**: Long-form motivic development across an entire
movement or piece. Beethoven 5th Symphony's 4-note motif
undergoes most of the techniques above across 30+ minutes.

**Pop**: Shorter motivic life. Motifs typically:
- Establish in verse (8 bars)
- Vary slightly in chorus or pre-chorus
- May return in bridge (transformed)
- Recur in final chorus (recognized)

### 8.4 Motif vs Hook

A motif is structural building material; a hook is the
memorable phrase intended to lodge in listener memory. They
overlap but serve different functions:

- Motif: Subtle, often subconscious; provides cohesion
- Hook: Foreground, designed to be remembered after one listen

A good pop song uses a motif as the seed of the hook, then
varies the motif in non-hook sections.

## SECTION 9 — APPLICATION GUIDE

### 9.1 Choosing Time Signature

Decision criteria:
- Genre conventions (4/4 default for most contemporary)
- Vocal phrase length (4/4 supports 4-8 bar phrases naturally)
- Rhythmic complexity desired (odd meters add complexity)
- Reference tracks in similar style

### 9.2 Choosing Groove

Decision criteria:
- Genre target (pop standard, funk pocket, hip-hop lay-back, etc.)
- Vocal style (rapped vs sung lays differently in groove)
- BPM (slow tempos benefit from ghost notes; fast tempos don't)
- Overall energy target

### 9.3 Choosing Form

Decision criteria:
- Story arc (does the lyric narrative require multiple distinct
  sections?)
- Energy curve target
- Genre conventions (K-pop multi-section, jazz AABA, EDM DJ form)
- Listener attention span (song length and section pacing)
- Modulation plans (does the song shift key for impact?)

### 9.4 Common Pitfalls

- **Section length without bar count**: Suno may compress or
  expand unpredictably; specify bar counts when needed
- **Energy curve too flat**: Verse and chorus at same density
  removes dynamic
- **Bridge that doesn't twist**: A bridge that just repeats
  chorus emotion is wasted real estate
- **Modulation without preparation**: Direct modulation without
  pivot or strong arrangement support sounds amateurish
- **Polyrhythm without separation**: Multiple polyrhythms layered
  without panning or volume separation become incomprehensible
- **Microtiming inconsistency**: Drummer shifting between push
  and lay-back within a song breaks pocket
- **Overusing dance breaks or bridges**: Too many "different"
  sections reduces the song's central identity

### §UE-1. Rhythm Groove Library

#### §UE-1.1 Standard Grooves by Genre

```
Pop (4/4, 120 BPM):
- Kick: 1, 3
- Snare: 2, 4
- Hat: 8th notes (continuous)

Hip-hop (4/4, 80-100 BPM):
- Kick: 1, 3.5, 4
- Snare: 2, 4
- Hat: 16th notes + occasional triplet (Migos flow)

EDM (4/4, 124-130 BPM):
- Kick: 1, 2, 3, 4 (four-on-the-floor)
- Snare/Clap: 2, 4
- Hat: 16th notes (open hat on 2.5, 4.5)

Trap (4/4, 70-90 BPM):
- Kick: variable, syncopated
- Snare: 3 (half-time feel)
- Hat: 16th + triplet rolls

Reggaeton (4/4, 90-100 BPM):
- Dembow: Boom-cha-boom-chick (1, 2.5, 3, 3.5)
- Hat: 8th

K-Pop Dance (4/4, 120-140 BPM):
- Mixed grooves (verse different from chorus)
- Trap verse → EDM chorus typical
```

#### §UE-1.2 Polyrhythm & Hemiola

```
Polyrhythm 3:2:
- Voice 1: 3 beats per measure
- Voice 2: 2 beats per measure
- Creates tension, modern feel

Hemiola:
- 3 beats grouped as 2 (or vice versa)
- Common in Latin, World music

Suno 어법:
- "polyrhythmic" / "syncopated" Style Box
- "tresillo" (3+3+2 pattern)
- "clave" (Latin pattern)
```


### §UE-2. Form Architecture 풀바디

#### §UE-2.1 Common Pop Forms

```
Form 1: Verse-Chorus-Verse-Chorus-Bridge-Chorus
- 가장 흔한 pop form
- 3:00-4:00 분량

Form 2: AABA (32-bar)
- Standard jazz / classic pop
- A=8, B=8 each

Form 3: VPCH (Verse-Pre-Chorus-Hook)
- Modern pop
- Pre-Chorus 추가 = 더 강한 build

Form 4: Intro-V-P-C-V-P-C-Bridge-C-Outro
- K-pop typical (more sections)
- Dance break / Rap verse 자리 추가

Form 5: Through-composed
- 클래식 / art pop
- Verse-Chorus 반복 없음
```

#### §UE-2.2 K-Pop Section Structure (bitwize 통합)

```
Intro → Verse 1 → Pre-Chorus → Chorus → Post-Chorus →
Verse 2 → Pre-Chorus → Chorus → Rap Verse → Bridge →
Dance Break → Final Chorus (key change up) → Outro
```

**Final chorus often modulates up a half-step** —
note `(key change up, maximum energy)` in parenthetical

## §UE-5. 현대 곡 구조 마이크로 패턴 풀바디

기존 §UE-2가 *전통 form* (VCVCBC / AABA / K-Pop multi-section 등)을
잡았다면, §UE-5는 *현대 마이크로 패턴*에 집중. 2020년대 곡 구조 흐름.

### §UE-5.1 Hook-Loop (트랩/드릴/모던 힙합)

**특성:**
- 1-2 bar hook이 *전체 곡 backbone*
- Verse / Chorus 경계 약함
- Hook 변형 박음 (정확 반복 X)
- 4분 미만 곡

**구조:**
```
[Intro 4 - Hook 변형]
[Verse 1 16]
[Hook 8]
[Verse 2 16]
[Hook 8]
[Verse 3 8] (선택)
[Outro - Hook fade]
```

**Suno 어법:**
```
"hook-loop structure with 2-bar repeated motif"
"hook-driven with verses circling back to chorus hook"
"single-hook architecture, no traditional bridge"
```

**적합 자리:**
- Trap / Drill / Phonk
- Modern Hip-Hop / Mumble Rap
- Drake-style "loose" 구조
- Hyperpop 단편

### §UE-5.2 Bridge-as-Climax (Adele lineage)

**특성:**
- Bridge가 *곡 최고점* (Final Chorus보다 강함)
- 일반적 bridge = 대비 / 휴식이 X
- 새 멜로디 + 새 키 + 새 lyric perspective

**구조:**
```
[Intro 4]
[Verse 1 8] (mid energy)
[Pre-Chorus 4]
[Chorus 8] (high energy)
[Verse 2 8]
[Pre-Chorus 4]
[Chorus 8]
[Bridge 16] ⭐ MAX ENERGY — modulation + new key
[Final Chorus 8] (echo, not climax)
[Outro 4]
```

**Suno 어법:**
```
"Adele-lineage structure with bridge-as-climax architecture"
"bridge peaks the song at +1 key with full ensemble entry"
"Final Chorus as resolution rather than climax"
```

**적합 자리:**
- Pop ballad / Soul / R&B 발라드
- 영화 OST / 시네마틱 팝
- Sam Smith / Adele / Florence + the Machine

### §UE-5.3 Pre-Chorus-Driven (Katy Perry lineage)

**특성:**
- Pre-Chorus가 *가장 강한 hook 자리*
- Chorus는 confirmation / Pre의 release
- Tension은 Pre / Resolution은 Chorus

**구조:**
```
[Intro 4]
[Verse 1 8]
[Pre-Chorus 8] ⭐ MAIN HOOK
[Chorus 8] (release / payoff)
[Verse 2 8]
[Pre-Chorus 8] ⭐
[Chorus 8]
[Bridge 8]
[Pre-Chorus 8] ⭐
[Chorus 8]
[Outro 4]
```

**Suno 어법:**
```
"pre-chorus driven structure with main hook in pre-section"
"chorus serves as confirmation rather than primary hook"
"tension build in pre-chorus, release in chorus"
```

**적합 자리:**
- 2010s 팝 anthem (Katy Perry "Firework")
- Modern pop with strong pre 라인
- Anthemic singer-songwriter

### §UE-5.4 Post-Chorus 어법 (Sia lineage)

**특성:**
- Chorus *직후* "second hook" 자리
- 가사 wordless 가능 ("yeah" / "oh" vocables)
- 두 가지 타입: *Attached* (코러스 hook 반복) / *Detached* (새 melody)

**구조 (Detached 타입 — "Chandelier" 결):**
```
[Verse]
[Pre-Chorus]
[Chorus] — main hook
[Post-Chorus] ⭐ — 새 melody, 같은 chord progression
[Verse]
...
```

**구조 (Attached 타입 — "Umbrella" 결):**
```
[Verse]
[Chorus] — "you can stand under my umbrella"
[Post-Chorus] — "ella ella ella ay ay ay" (hook 반복 + vocable)
[Verse]
...
```

**Suno 어법:**
```
"post-chorus hook with vocable repetition after main chorus"
"detached post-chorus with new melody and same chord progression"
"attached post-chorus with hook word repetition and vocables"
```

**적합 자리:**
- Sia / Rihanna / 모던 팝 ear-worm
- K-pop (post-chorus 거의 표준)
- EDM-pop crossover

### §UE-5.5 Through-Composed (Bohemian Rhapsody lineage)

**특성:**
- 섹션 *반복 X*
- 연속 새 material
- Verse-Chorus 결 부재
- Prog / Art / 영화 OST

**구조:**
```
[Section A] (intro + 첫 절)
[Section B] (다른 mood)
[Section C] (climax / operatic)
[Section D] (rock interlude)
[Section E] (return to opening mood)
```

**Suno 어법:**
```
"through-composed structure with no section repetition"
"continuous evolution through five distinct sections"
"prog-rock art-song architecture with operatic climax"
```

**적합 자리:**
- Queen "Bohemian Rhapsody"
- Yorushika / Vaundy 일부 곡
- 영화 OST / 게임 OST
- Art pop / Prog

### §UE-5.6 Anti-Drop (EDM 역설)

**특성:**
- EDM build-up *후* drop 자리에 *정적* / *minimal*
- 청자 예상 깨기
- Bridge 자리에 박음

**구조:**
```
[Intro 16]
[Verse 8]
[Build-up 8]
[Drop 16] (full energy)
[Verse 8]
[Build-up 8]
[Anti-Drop 8] ⭐ — silence or single vocal
[Drop 16] (return to full energy)
[Outro 8]
```

**Suno 어법:**
```
"anti-drop section with sudden silence after build-up"
"unexpected minimal section where drop would be expected"
"tension subversion with stripped vocal in place of EDM drop"
```

**적합 자리:**
- Modern EDM with experimental edge
- Skrillex / Flume / Porter Robinson
- Pop-EDM crossover

### §UE-5.7 Pre-Intro (2025 트렌드)

**특성:**
- 0:00에 *hook teaser* 박음 (formal intro 전)
- 청자 0초 캡쳐
- TikTok / Shorts era 어법

**구조:**
```
[Pre-Intro 2] ⭐ — quick hook teaser or beat drop + silence
[Intro 4-8]
[Verse 1]
...
```

**Suno 어법:**
```
"pre-intro hook teaser at 0:00 before formal intro"
"instant hook capture in first 2 seconds"
"sample-style pre-intro preceding band entry"
```

**적합 자리:**
- TikTok-friendly pop
- Short-form social music
- 모던 K-pop 2024+

### §UE-5.8 Verse-Refrain (Folk / Indie)

**특성:**
- Chorus 자리에 *single-line refrain*
- Verse 강조 (story-driven)
- Bob Dylan / 폴크 lineage

**구조:**
```
[Verse 1 12] (story)
[Refrain 2] — single line repeating title
[Verse 2 12]
[Refrain 2]
[Bridge 4]
[Verse 3 12]
[Refrain 2]
[Outro 4]
```

**Suno 어법:**
```
"verse-refrain folk structure with single-line refrain"
"story-driven verses with title refrain repeated"
"Dylan-lineage long verse with short refrain return"
```

**적합 자리:**
- 폴크 / 컨트리 storytelling
- 인디 singer-songwriter
- 시적 / 문학적 가사

### §UE-5.9 Layered Build (Cinematic / 영화 OST)

**특성:**
- 전체 곡이 *gradual build-up*
- 매 섹션 layer 추가
- Catharsis at Final Chorus
- Dynamic range 막대

**구조:**
```
[Intro 8] — piano alone
[Verse 1 8] — + vocal dry
[Pre-Chorus 4] — + strings entering soft
[Chorus 1 8] — + soft drums + harmony
[Verse 2 8] — full band entry
[Pre-Chorus 4] — full strings + tension
[Chorus 2 8] — full energy
[Bridge 8] — strip back to piano + voice
[Final Chorus 16] — MAX with key change + cymbal swell + ad-libs
[Outro 8] — decay
```

**Suno 어법:**
```
"cinematic layered build with gradual instrument entry"
"massive dynamic range from intimate piano to anthem chorus"
"film-anthem architecture with bridge strip-down before climax"
```

**적합 자리:**
- 영화 OST / 시네마틱 팝
- Adele / Sam Smith big ballad
- Coldplay-lineage anthem
- K-pop OST

### §UE-5.10 K-Pop B-Section 어법

**특성:**
- Bridge 자리에 *완전 다른 곡* 박음
- 새 chord progression / 새 BPM 가능
- 새 vocal style (랩 verse 등)
- 곡 안 곡 (song within song)

**구조:**
```
[Verse 1 8]
[Pre-Chorus 4]
[Chorus 8]
[Verse 2 8]
[Pre-Chorus 4]
[Chorus 8]
[B-Section 16] ⭐ — completely different vibe, new BPM possible
  - Rap verse with different vocal style
  - Or experimental section with new chord progression
[Final Chorus 8] (often modulated)
[Outro 4]
```

**Suno 어법:**
```
"K-pop B-section architecture with bridge as song-within-song"
"experimental bridge with completely different vibe and BPM"
"rap verse insertion between second chorus and final chorus"
```

**적합 자리:**
- 모던 K-pop 4세대
- SM Entertainment SMP 어법
- aespa / ATEEZ / Stray Kids


## §UE-6. 가사 ↔ 음악 구조 ↔ Suno 프롬프트 3축 매핑

운영자 핵심 통찰:
> *"가사에 따라 음악적 구성이라든가 어떻게 기승전결만 있는 게 아니라 뭐
>  훅만 감는 것도 있고 그런 전체적인 음악의 구조도 뭐 간주가 들어가는
>  게 좋다 이런 것까지... 결국 Suno 프롬프트로 직결되어 그게 표현되냐가
>  다 중요하니까."*

### §UE-6.1 가사 결 → 곡 구조 매핑

```
가사 결: "단편적 모먼트 / 단일 이미지"
→ 곡 구조: Hook-Loop (§UE-5.1) / Pre-Intro (§UE-5.7)
→ Suno 어법: "hook-loop structure" / "instant hook capture"

가사 결: "서사 / 스토리텔링"
→ 곡 구조: Verse-Refrain (§UE-5.8) / Through-Composed (§UE-5.5)
→ Suno 어법: "verse-refrain folk structure" / "through-composed"

가사 결: "감정 정점 / 폭발"
→ 곡 구조: Bridge-as-Climax (§UE-5.2) / Layered Build (§UE-5.9)
→ Suno 어법: "bridge-as-climax architecture" / "cinematic layered build"

가사 결: "긴장 + 해방"
→ 곡 구조: Pre-Chorus-Driven (§UE-5.3) / Standard VCVCBC
→ Suno 어법: "pre-chorus driven structure"

가사 결: "반복 + 변형 + ear-worm"
→ 곡 구조: Post-Chorus 어법 (§UE-5.4)
→ Suno 어법: "post-chorus hook with vocable repetition"

가사 결: "두 시점 / 두 곡 융합"
→ 곡 구조: K-Pop B-Section (§UE-5.10)
→ Suno 어법: "K-pop B-section architecture"

가사 결: "예상 깨기 / 반전"
→ 곡 구조: Anti-Drop (§UE-5.6)
→ Suno 어법: "anti-drop section with sudden silence"
```

### §UE-6.2 음악 컨셉 → 가사 구조 매핑

```
음악 컨셉: "트랩 / 드릴 / 모던 힙합"
→ 가사 구조: Hook-Loop 어법
   - 1-2 bar hook 변형
   - Verse 16 bars (storytelling)
   - Bridge X (또는 짧은 ad-lib section)

음악 컨셉: "발라드 / R&B 발라드"
→ 가사 구조: Bridge-as-Climax
   - Verse: setup (낮은 energy)
   - Pre: tension build
   - Chorus: payoff
   - Bridge: NEW lyric perspective + modulation
   - Final Chorus: echo / resolution

음악 컨셉: "댄스 / EDM-pop"
→ 가사 구조: Post-Chorus 어법
   - Verse: setup
   - Pre: build
   - Chorus: main hook
   - Post-Chorus: ear-worm vocable

음악 컨셉: "K-pop 4세대"
→ 가사 구조: B-Section + Post-Chorus
   - Verse / Pre / Chorus / Post (반복)
   - B-Section (rap verse / new vibe)
   - Final Chorus (key up)

음악 컨셉: "영화 OST / 시네마틱"
→ 가사 구조: Layered Build
   - Intro: piano only
   - Verse 1: + vocal dry
   - Pre: + strings
   - Chorus 1: + drums + harmony
   - Verse 2: full band
   - Bridge: strip back
   - Final Chorus: MAX + key up
```

```
   - EDM/댄스 → "Synth intro 8 bars?"

```

## §UE-8. 멜로디 컨투어 × 하모니 × 연출 — 한 몸 적재 체계

### §UE-8.0 진단 — 왜 이게 필요한가

기존 SOP는 멜로디를 **코드 진행 + 추상 형용사("end-accented")**로만
다뤘다. 둘 다 Suno에게 *간접 신호*다 — 코드는 화성만 정하지 음정 운동을
안 정하고, 형용사는 "느낌"만 줘서 실제 음 운동은 통계 평균치(=순차진행=
올드)로 채워진다. **음정이 어떻게 움직이는지를 지정하는 레이어가 통째로
비어 있었다.** "멜로디가 계속 구리다"의 근본 원인.

이 §은 그 빈 레이어를 채운다. 핵심 명제:

> **컨투어 · 하모니 · 연출은 따로 노는 게 아니라 한 몸이다.**
> - 컨투어(음정 운동) = 설계도 — "음이 어디로 가나"
> - 하모니(화성) = 받침 — "그 음이 어느 코드톤에 착지하나"
> - 연출(보컬 큐) = 시공 — "그 음을 어떻게 부르나"
>
> 셋을 따로 박으면 반쪽이 되고 Suno가 평균치로 메운다.

### §UE-8.1 컨투어 이론 (적재용 압축)

**컨투어 = 시간에 따른 멜로디의 형태·방향** (상승/하강/정체).

| 운동 | 정의 | 정서 | 적합 자리 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Conjunct (순차) | 2도 이내 인접 이동 | 매끄러움, 부르기 쉬움, 기억 쉬움 | Verse 깔림, 담담 |
| Disjunct (도약) | 3도 이상 점프 | 각짐, 극적, 흥분·놀람 | Hook, 강조 |
| Ascending (상승) | 피치 상승 | 텐션·에너지·긍정·고양 | Pre→Chorus build |
| Descending (하강) | 피치 하강 | 해결·이완·체념·하강텐션 | 코웃음, release |
| Static (정체) | tonic 주변 평탄 | 긴장 축적, 도도, 담담 | Verse 읊조림 |

**연동 핵심 (voice-leading 정설):**
1. **도약은 코드 톤에 착지** → 화성이 받쳐줘서 안정. (코드톤 아닌 데 도약
   하면 불안정 → Suno가 헤맴) 멜로디가 코드의 아르페지오일 땐 도약이
   "완결"로 들림 (반대방향 해결 없이도 OK).
2. **순차는 passing/tension tone**으로 코드 사이를 메움.
3. **도약 후 반대방향 step**으로 해결 (균형). leap → step.
4. **단일 peak note** — 최고음은 곡에서 1회, 강박(1·3박)에 배치.
5. **tendency tone 해결** — leading tone → tonic, ♭6 → 5 등.

### §UE-8.2 3층 표기 체계 (글자 체계 개정 — 산문 폐기)

기존 산문 어법 폐기:
```
❌ "melody stays monotone around the tonic then drops a minor
    third on the last word for a smirk" (산문, 비효율, Suno 평균치)
```

신규 3층 표기:

**Layer A — 컨투어 정식 태그 (Lyrics box, 섹션 첫 줄)**
Suno가 직접 받는 검증된 태그. 섹션 첫 줄 = 멜로디 가중치 최대.
```
[Ascending melody] / [Descending melody] / [Emotional climax]
[Flattened tone] (♭3/♭6/♭7 블루지·다크) / [Falling tension]
[Ascending progression] (피치·키·화성 lift 전진)
```

**Layer B — 화성 연동 표기 (CREATE Style box)**
컨투어 도약이 *어느 코드톤*에 착지하는지 명시 (이론 §UE-8.1 적재).
```
"verse melody lands on chord tones of F#m9 staying low,
 chorus hook leaps up to the 5th then steps down,
 pre-chorus climbs stepwise toward the dominant"
```

**Layer C — 度단위 보조 (Style box 1줄, 정밀 자리만)**
메인 아닌 보조. 산문 금지, 1구절.
```
"minor-third drop on phrase-ends"
```

### §UE-8.3 연출 적재 (건성 탈출 — C-99~102 강화)

**진단**: [Singing:]에 *길게 산문*으로 박는 게 건성의 정체. Suno는
짧은 실행형 큐를 더 정확히 받는다.

```
❌ 산문: [Singing: warm legato phrasing with slight rubato on
   phrase-ends, expressive vibrato blooming on sustained vowels...]
✅ 짧은 실행형 큐 (섹션 첫 줄):
   (whispered) (belted) (spoken word) (harmonized) (ad-lib)
   (falsetto) (building intensity) (stripped back) (half-time feel)
```

**★ 연출 = 컨투어 실현 (한 몸의 핵심):**
보컬 다이내믹이 컨투어의 상승/하강을 *부르는 법*으로 실현한다.
```
상승 컨투어  → (building intensity) + crescendo + belt
하강 컨투어  → (stripped back) + decrescendo
정체 컨투어  → deadpan, conversational, even dynamics
도약(climax) → (belted) + 강박 배치
```

### §UE-8.4 섹션별 통합 템플릿 (컨투어+하모니+연출 한 줄)

재사용 템플릿. 각 섹션 = 컨투어 태그 + 화성 연동 + 연출 큐.

```
[Verse]
  컨투어: static/conjunct around tonic (담담 깔림)
  하모니: lands on chord tones, Aeolian vamp i-v-bVI-bVII
  연출: (conversational) close mic, even dynamics
  → Lyrics: [Verse] + [Singing: conversational, close mic]
  → CREATE: "verse melody static around tonic on chord tones"

[Pre-Chorus]
  컨투어: ascending stepwise (build)
  하모니: climbs toward dominant, secondary dominant 텐션
  연출: (building intensity) crescendo
  → Lyrics: [Pre-Chorus] [Ascending melody] + [Singing: building intensity]
  → CREATE: "pre-chorus climbs stepwise toward the dominant"

[Chorus]
  컨투어: leap to chord tone + ascending (폭발)
  하모니: bIII or IV lift (relative major 청량)
  연출: (belted) building intensity, peak note 강박
  → Lyrics: [Chorus] [Emotional climax] + [Singing: belt, building intensity]
  → CREATE: "chorus hook leaps to the 5th of the bIII chord, ascending lift"

[Bridge]
  컨투어: narrow range OR contrast contour
  하모니: static harmony (pedal) OR departure
  연출: (stripped back) + 끝 microtonal bend
  → Lyrics: [Bridge] + [Singing: stripped back] + [1/8 Tone Pitch Bends]
  → CREATE: "bridge narrow contour over tonic pedal"

[Final Chorus]
  컨투어: chorus 컨투어 + peak note 1회 + 반음 위
  하모니: same lift + half-step modulation (옵션)
  연출: (belted) fullest
  → Lyrics: [Final Chorus] [Emotional climax] + [Singing: belt fullest]
```

### §UE-8.5 Suno 3-pass 정합 (= 우리 CREATE/COVER)

외부 검증 (CometAPI): V5 작곡 아키텍처는 3-pass로 일관성 유지.
```
idea pass    (코드 베드 + 컨투어 골격)  = CREATE (bone, 09 §3)
lyric pass   (가사 컨투어 매칭, 강세·음절) = Lyrics box 작성
performance  (보컬·다이내믹·텍스처)       = COVER (texture, 09 §3)
```
→ 우리 CREATE/COVER 분리가 이미 V5 정합. 컨투어는 CREATE(bone) 쪽에
박힌다. COVER엔 거의 안 박힘 (텍스처라).

### §UE-8.7 자동 점검 (출력 직전)

```
1. 컨투어 태그 — 섹션 첫 줄 박혔나? (Layer A)
2. 화성 연동 — CREATE에 도약 착지 코드톤 명시? (Layer B)

4. 연출=컨투어 실현 — 상승/하강에 맞는 다이내믹 큐?
5. peak note — 최고음 1회 강박?
6. 표기:
   🎼 컨투어×하모니×연출: 태그 [N] / 화성연동 ✅ / 연출큐 [M] / 통과
```

### §UE-8.8 금지 (DON'Ts)

```
❌ 컨투어를 度단위 산문으로 길게 (Suno 평균치 + 토큰낭비)
❌ 컨투어만 박고 화성 연동 안 함 (도약 착지점 불명 → 헤맴)
❌ 연출을 컨투어와 따로 (다이내믹이 음 운동과 안 맞음)
❌ [Singing:] 산문 떡칠 (짧은 실행형 큐로)
❌ peak note 여러 번 (최고음 남발 → 임팩트 분산)
```
