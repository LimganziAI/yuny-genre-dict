# SIM-20260612 TEAM CORPUS QUALITATIVE ROUND 2

## purpose
Continue lyric-only training round focused on writing quality: situation, description, vocabulary, metaphor source, section movement, and speaker posture.

## source
User team video corpus. Full lyrics are not pasted here. This log records derived craft analysis only.

## key finding
The stronger team lyrics do not work because of abstract poetic language. They work because a concrete scene produces a speaker, and the speaker's actions make the metaphor necessary.

## representative reverse-briefs

### 1. 바람, 풀내음 그리고
Likely situation:
An evening alley pause where smell, warm air, and small neighborhood sounds trigger a name and a half-confession.

Writing behavior:
- sensory entrance: light, air, hand, alley, smell
- object/sound actions: dinner smell leaks, bicycle chain unwinds, the speaker stops
- metaphor source: wind loosens the name; green scent spreads into memory
- hook function: a name diffuses through the scene, not an explained longing
- craft lesson: sensory detail should perform the emotion before the speaker names it

YUNY must learn:
Start with one perceivable sensation, then let it pull the relation into the lyric.

### 2. 마리 온 더 루프탑
Likely situation:
A rooftop confession that fails by one step, one beat, one word.

Writing behavior:
- repeated measure language creates structure
- body hesitation is the plot
- setting objects are functional: stairs, door, railing, rooftop edge, wind
- hook function: distance measure becomes emotional refrain
- final shift: hesitation becomes self-knowledge rather than simple failure

YUNY must learn:
Small physical distance can carry the whole dramatic arc if each section changes what that distance means.

### 3. 런닝메이트
Likely situation:
A speaker runs beside someone whose pace is slightly too fast, hiding exhaustion as affection or pride.

Writing behavior:
- body verbs carry emotion: tying shoes, matching watch, breathing hard, sweating, stumbling
- comic inner monologue prevents sentimentality
- object-action coupling is strong: shoelaces, watch, streetlights, pavement
- hook function: running pace becomes relationship tempo
- V2 potential: the other person's glance changes the speaker's embarrassment into attachment

YUNY must learn:
Romance can be written through mismatched tempo rather than direct confession.

### 4. Trust, but verify
Likely situation:
Jealous phone-check monologue disguised as rational calm.

Writing behavior:
- spoken denial drives the lyric
- timestamps, replies, profile changes, and checking behavior become evidence
- humor comes from contradiction between said calm and obsessive action
- hook function: a slogan of control exposes insecurity
- voice is more important than ornate imagery

YUNY must learn:
A lyric can be powered by self-contradicting speech rhythm, not only images.

### 5. 충고 고마워
Likely situation:
A speaker rejects unwanted advice with dry sarcasm and boundary-setting.

Writing behavior:
- direct speech posture is the song's engine
- metaphor is social and tactile, not decorative
- everyday objects support tone: can, hand, shade, distance
- hook function: polite phrase becomes a weapon
- vocabulary palette is sharp, warm-bitter, conversational

YUNY must learn:
Plain speech can be the sharpest lyric when the social posture is clear.

### 6. 가산디지토리안과 구로구
Likely situation:
A competent office worker becomes helpless at home with childcare or domestic care.

Writing behavior:
- contrast engine: workplace competence versus private uncertainty
- objects are practical and high-stakes: office light, bag, formula ratio, home task
- emotional scale flips: small domestic task feels cosmic
- hook function: caring becomes an epic mission
- craft lesson: scale mismatch can create both humor and tenderness

YUNY must learn:
A song can become moving by making a small action feel enormous to the speaker.

### 7. 해에게서 해에게
Likely situation:
A seaside dawn lyric where natural motion becomes address, renewal, or passing from one light to another.

Writing behavior:
- image sequence is coherent: dawn, water, sand, salt, lighthouse, gull, shell, horizon
- metaphor family stays within sea/light
- hook likely works by expansion rather than punchline
- danger: this lane can become generic if not tied to a speaker's particular action

YUNY must learn:
Lyrical imagery needs a stable world, but also a human action or relation to avoid postcard writing.

## cross-song craft rule
A good lyric line should answer at least one of these:

1. What did the speaker notice?
2. What did the speaker do with the object?
3. What changed in the body?
4. What social posture is being performed?
5. What section pressure increased?
6. What metaphor family is being extended?
7. What did V2 newly reveal?
8. What changes in the final repeat?

## expressive vocabulary rule
Team-corpus style is not maximal poetry. It alternates:
- plain spoken admission
- concrete object action
- short sensory pressure
- repeated hook phrase
- small comic contradiction
- final residue

YUNY should not decorate every line. Some lines must be plain enough to make the image lines land.
