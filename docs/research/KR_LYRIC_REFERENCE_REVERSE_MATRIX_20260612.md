## purpose
Build a reference-analysis layer from current charts, long-loved songs, and critically discussed K-pop/indie/pop songs.

Do not quote or copy lyrics. Use references only as craft-function evidence.

## source lanes
1. current chart lane: recent Circle/Melon-adjacent popularity and 2026 chart-toppers
2. endurance lane: long-charting and highly liked Korean songs
3. critic lane: songs repeatedly discussed as strong K-pop or pop craft
4. team lane: user/team corpus remains the primary taste baseline

## craft lanes to test

### chart-current immediacy
Often works through direct hook identity, short recognizability, social repeat, clean sonic-linguistic fit, and high concept clarity. Use for hook-cell, chant, dance-pop, and short-form impact.

### long-loved narrative residue
Often works through a simple emotional center plus a durable object, season, address, or memory structure. Use for ballad, indie pop, chamber R&B, and emotional OST-adjacent writing.

### critically praised K-pop architecture
Often works through section contrast, concept clarity, performance identity, compressed hook wording, and arrangement-event lyric fit. Use for hybrid pop, performance-driven songs, and genre transformations.

### team-corpus scene writing
Works through character posture, object-action coupling, plain speech with image pressure, low-to-medium repeat, V2 angle, and small final shift. This remains the home baseline.

## runtime rule
Before serious Korean lyric generation, choose one reference lane and one team-corpus lane. The song must not become a reference imitation; it should borrow method, pressure, and function.

## acceptance addition
A lyric fails if all references collapse into the same tone. Current chart energy, endurance lyric memory, critic architecture, and team-corpus scene writing must remain distinguishable.
