# KR_LYRIC_DRAFT_ORIGIN_RESEARCH_SYNTHESIS_20260612

## purpose
This file converts uploaded papers, corpus notes, and system failure cases into operational rules for the YUNY Suno 8-field Project.

## integrated evidence
- Korean prosodic phonology: phonological structure and syntactic structure do not always map one-to-one; lyric line breaks should respect phonological phrase and breath, not only written syntax.
- Korean pronunciation education: pronunciation affects communication across speaking/listening/reading/writing; in song, intelligibility is a lyric-quality gate, not a final ornament.
- Korean phoneme/popular-lyric research: lyric writing can be theorized through vocal/acoustic features; writers note open vowels and consonant choices matter in high notes and genre delivery.
- Korean syllable frequency research: everyday Korean uses a familiar subset of possible syllables; conversational hooks should prefer mouth-familiar syllable shapes unless character/genre demands stiffness.
- Literature education research: literary language works through human, event, background, theme, and cultural vocabulary; lyric images must be situated in person/event/background, not pasted on top.
- Song/poem intertextuality: song and poem interact through sound and text; lyric must survive as language while fitting musical expression.
- AI creative-writing research and supplied reports: LLM outputs often become smooth, generic, over-structured, and socially/genre-context shallow unless given strong scaffolds and revision gates.
- User/team lyric corpus: stronger outputs are scene-specific, speech-act driven, and less repetitive than generic K-pop averages.

## system implication
More data does not fix Korean lyrics unless generation order changes.
The system must force:
speaker pressure → ugly monologue → hook mining → lyric carving → morpheme repair → phoneme repair → cue pass.

## forbidden use
Do not paste source lyrics, copyrighted song lines, dialogue, or corpus lines into outputs. Use derived constraints only.
