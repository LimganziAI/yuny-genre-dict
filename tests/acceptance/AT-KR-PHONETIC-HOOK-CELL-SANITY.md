# AT-KR-PHONETIC-HOOK-CELL-SANITY

## purpose
Prevent rhythm-first Korean hooks from being judged by narrative prose rules, while also preventing broken prose from being excused as a hook.

## trigger
Use when lyric mode is phonetic hook, dance chorus, chant refrain, post-chorus, performance hook, or section-level Mode B.

## required decision before hook writing
```text
HOOK MODE: phonetic / chant / slogan / chopped sentence / vocable
WHY: section function + genre + listener action
REPEAT DOSE: low / medium / high
MEANING LOAD: low / medium / high
```

## pass criteria
- The repeat cell is easy to sing after one listen.
- Vowels land cleanly on stressed beats.
- Korean fragments are intentional musical cells, not accidental broken prose.
- Repetition creates momentum, not filler.
- The hook has one clear identity: sound, slogan, action, or confession.
- Meaning load fits the section. Dance hooks may be lighter; narrative choruses need more meaning.
- The hook can survive with fewer words if the groove carries it.

## fail criteria
- The model forces a full narrative sentence into a chant slot.
- The hook repeats words without rhythmic or emotional gain.
- The phrase is grammatically broken but not musically justified.
- The vowel endings fight the intended melody contour.
- The hook sounds like translated English pop filler.
- The hook loses the song's central promise.

## required self-test table
After draft hook:

| hook phrase | vowel landing | rhythm cell | meaning load | repeat fatigue | decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

## repair rule
If the hook fails, do not rewrite the whole song. Repair the hook cell first, then check whether verse/pre still lead into it.
